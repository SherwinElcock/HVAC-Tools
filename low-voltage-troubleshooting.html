SERVICE CALL CHECKLIST

Low-Voltage Troubleshooting

Tracing the 24V control circuit: R, C, W, Y, G, O/B. Work transformer → stat → board → safeties → load, checking for 24V at each stop.

Wire reference

RPower from transformer (24V hot)
CCommon — completes circuit to transformer
YCooling call — energizes contactor
WHeat call
GFan call
O/BReversing valve (heat pumps) — O=cool, B=heat

Troubleshooting sequence

  1. Confirm the transformer
    Meter across R and C at the board, no call active.
    ~24-28VTransformer is good, move to step 2.
    0VTransformer dead or fused output blown. Nothing downstream will work until this is fixed.
  2. Confirm the stat is sending the call
    With a call active, check R to Y (or R to W / R to G) at the equipment board.
    24VCall is reaching the board. Move to step 3.
    0VBreak is between stat and board — check thermostat wiring, loose terminals, or the stat itself.
  3. Confirm the board is passing the call through
    Check the output side — Y to C right at the board's output terminal.
    24VBoard is passing the call. Move to step 4.
    0V (but 24V on input)Board itself, or a safety wired into it (float switch, high-limit), is holding the call.
  4. Check the load
    Contactor coil terminals, gas valve, etc.
    24V present, load not energizingLoad itself is bad — replace contactor/coil or valve.
    0V at loadSomething in that leg (float switch, high-limit, pressure switch) is breaking it before it arrives. Check each safety in series — 24V across an open switch, 0V across a closed one.
Key principle: work in one direction — transformer, then stat, then board, then safeties, then load — checking for 24V at each stop. Wherever voltage disappears is the problem.

Common gotchas

  • Condensate float switch tripped — kills Y (sometimes R) on many systems. The most common no-cool call.
  • Missing C wire — older systems without one may run stats on battery or power-stealing, which can cause erratic behavior.
  • Shorted thermostat wire — bare wires touching can blow the transformer fuse or trip the breaker. Check for a short in the wire run before condemning the transformer on 0V at R-C.
