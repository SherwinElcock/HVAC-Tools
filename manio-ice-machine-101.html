How It Works Not Making Ice — Checklist

1 Freeze Cycle

Compressor running · hot gas valve closed · pump circulating

The compressor runs continuously, pushing refrigerant through the condenser (cooled by the fan motor) and into the evaporator plate. On the water side, the pump pulls from the sump and sprays it over the evaporator grid. Water freezes onto the plate in layers as it recirculates — each pass adds thickness until the cubes reach the size the control is set for.

Ice thickness is sensed by a probe (or a fixed freeze timer on simpler controls). Once satisfied, the board ends the freeze cycle and starts harvest.

2 Harvest Cycle

Hot gas solenoid opens · gear motor engages · pump off (or reduced)

The hot gas solenoid (.1A) opens, routing hot discharge gas directly into the evaporator instead of the condenser. This warms the plate just enough to break the ice-to-metal bond. The gear motor (this model uses a gear-motor–assisted harvest) helps release and guide the sheet so it drops cleanly instead of hanging up on the grid.

If the hot gas solenoid or gear motor doesn't energize, the sheet won't release — this reads to the operator as "not making ice" even though freeze looked fine.

3 Water Fill

Float / water level control tops off the sump

As cubes drop and the sump empties, a float or electronic water-level control opens the water inlet to refill for the next freeze cycle. Scale buildup on the float or probe is a common, easy-to-miss cause of erratic fill.
